Where does it come from? no frequency measured yet

The chatter frequency tells you which part of the system is vibrating. The highlighted band matches your last measurement.

< 100 HzMachine structure, workpiece, fixtureLarge, soft parts move: column, table, thin plates on the magnetic chuck, long clamping towers. Speed changes help little because the pockets are very dense. Stiffen the setup, support thin walls, reduce ae. This is the only range where Heidenhain ACC (option 145) can act, because it works through the feed drives.
100–600 HzSpindle, heavy holders, large cuttersFace mills, insert cutters and long extensions on the spindle bearings. Speed tuning works when the tooth-pass frequency is close to fc (low pockets). Otherwise reduce ap, use a stiffer or shorter holder.
600 Hz – 5 kHzTool and holder bendingThe typical case for end mills and ball mills with long overhang. Speed tuning works best here. Shorter overhang raises fn with 1/L² and stiffness with 1/L³.
> 5 kHzSmall tools, cutting edge, thin wallsOften squealing from rubbing: feed per tooth too low or a worn edge. Check fz first, then edge prep and tool wear. Thin ribs in the mould can also ring here.

What to try, in this order

  1. Spindle speed to a pocketMeasure fc, pick the nearest pocket from the table, cut again and re-measure. fc moves a little with the speed, so one or two iterations are normal.
  2. Feed per tooth not too smallBelow roughly 0.02–0.03 mm (more for honed inserts) the edge rubs instead of cutting, which produces high squeal. At small ae the real chip is thinner than fz, so rubbing starts earlier. Keep fz when you change n: F scales with n.
  3. Less radial engagement aeFewer teeth in the cut raise the stability limit. Trochoidal and HPC strategies use small ae with large ap.
  4. Shorter overhangUse the overhang calculator. Every mm counts with the third power.
  5. HolderShrink fit: stiffest. Hydraulic: softer but damps better. For long reach consider a damped extension.
  6. Variable pitch or helix cutterUnequal tooth spacing breaks the phase between teeth for a whole frequency band.
  7. Workpiece and fixtureSupport thin walls, add clamping points, check the magnetic plate contact and pole coverage.
  8. ACC (only below about 100 Hz)Set column ACC = Y for the tool in the tool table if option 145 is enabled. Ask the machine builder about axis tuning.

Mains hum peaks are labelled and never picked as chatter. Very low frequencies are mostly handling noise.

How the analysis works

  1. Spectrum32 768-point FFT with Hann window, about 1.5 Hz resolution, averaged over the capture.
  2. PeaksLocal maxima at least 8 dB above a running noise floor.
  3. Harmonics removedPeaks at multiples of the tooth-pass frequency n·z/60 or the spindle frequency n/60 are forced vibration, not chatter.
  4. ChatterThe strongest remaining peak. Sidebands at fc ± tooth-pass frequency raise the confidence.
  5. Speedsn = 60·fc / (z·(N+1)). Phone microphones are not calibrated, so levels are relative; frequencies are accurate.
